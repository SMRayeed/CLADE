\section{Page Edits}
\label{app:edits}

The abstention test compares different facts: the family-only questions are not the same facts as the silent ones. The page edits remove this difference. They ask each question three times, with three versions of one page, and change nothing else.

\paragraph{Which questions.}
We start from named questions, whose page names the answer. In PrimeKG we keep the 130 whose answer has a MONDO parent, since the family edit needs a parent name. In GloBI we keep the 255 named questions whose answer has a genus.

\paragraph{Three versions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Original}: the page as retrieved. It names the answer.
\item \emph{Family}: every mention of the answer, and of each of its alternative names, becomes the name of its immediate parent (the MONDO parent, or the genus). In GloBI we also replace the abbreviated form (\emph{A.~plantaginifolia}).
\item \emph{Removed}: every sentence that mentions the answer is deleted.
\end{itemize}
We replace whole words only. If the answer has several parents, we use the shortest name, and we never use a parent that is itself one of the options. We drop a question if the answer is still mentioned after the edit, or if nothing is left after removal.
For example, a page that says ``\emph{\dots used to treat familial hyperlipidemia}'' becomes ``\emph{\dots used to treat hyperlipidemia}'' in the family version, and loses that sentence in the removed version. The question, the options, their letters and the model stay the same. Each prompt is cached under a hash of its exact text, so an edited page can never reuse the answer of the original.

\paragraph{What the comparison shows.}
The family and removed versions both lack the answer. The only difference is that the family version keeps the family name in place of the answer. If the family name had no effect, the two versions would give the same outcomes. \Cref{tab:edit_models} shows that they do not: in every model and both domains, the family version draws fewer declines than the removed version.

\paragraph{Pooled effects.}
\Cref{tab:edit_pooled} pools the three models, averaging each question over models first. Against the removed version, the family name cuts declines by 17.4 points in PrimeKG and 27.7 in GloBI, and raises both wrong and correct answers. Against the original, the family version loses many correct answers and gains wrong ones. So the family name lets the model guess among the family members without telling it which one is meant.
